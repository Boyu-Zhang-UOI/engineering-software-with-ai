Rubrics ​

Scoring guides for every graded component except the oral exams (Oral Exams: Scoring), the quizzes (Due-Date Quizzes: Scoring) and the graduate work (graduate/). Each row is something a grader can see in a repository, a CI run, a PR or your writing.

How to read these tables. Each rubric has criteria (rows) and four levels (columns). The number in bold is the points for that level.

LevelMeans
ExemplaryEverything asked for, done carefully, with evidence anyone can check. Beyond what most students do
ProficientEverything asked for, done well. This is a good result
DevelopingStarted, but important parts are missing, unverified or unclear
MissingNot there

Graders pick the level that best fits each row. If a row falls between two levels, they use the lower one and say what would lift it. Weights and how points turn into your grade are in the assessment README.

Three rules apply to every rubric.

  1. Evidence, not assertion. "Tests pass" earns credit when there's a CI run, a command and its output, or a commit a grader can check out. Claims about code, agents and tools are tagged observed or inferred; an inferred claim presented as fact is a Developing-level mistake.
  2. Honest limitations never cost points. A known issue you name is worth more than one a grader finds. A documented blocker (the exact error, what you tried, the fallback you switched to) earns partial credit where the rubric says so.
  3. Rubric levels describe the work, not misconduct. Work that seems to break the AI-use rules or seems fabricated is handled under Academic integrity, and no score changes until the University process is complete.

Labs and assignments ​

35% of the course for CS [4xxx], 30% for CS [5xxx]. Weeks 1–13 are each scored out of 20 points: the Lab checkoff (5) and the take-home assignment (15). Your lowest week is dropped. The PR round adds 20 points and is never dropped. Each week's lab.md ends with an assignment section whose rubric summary says what each row below means that week (for example, "Verification" is precision and recall against the defect key in week 9, and Cohen's κ in week 12).

Lab checkoff (5 points) ​

Staff check the week's in-lab deliverable during the Lab's checkoff block (or earlier, if you finish the core early) and record complete, partial or missing. The criteria are at the end of the lab's Core parts. Staff check the evidence (a passing test run, a trace line, a commit, a diff) and ask one question about it. Oral exam weeks: if your exam slot falls in your Lab, show the checkoff in office hours that week instead, for full credit; it isn't an absence (week 8; week 14's checkoff is feedback only).

CompletePartialMissing
5 · Every checkoff criterion met, with the evidence on screen, and you can answer the question about it3 · Most criteria met, or met but you can't yet answer the question; or blocked, with the exact error and what you tried0 · Not shown, or absent without arrangement (make-ups)

Week 9's review freeze. Your five reviews and the AI reviewer's output are graded as they stood at your freeze (default 11:59 pm two days after the Lab; grace tokens don't extend it), and the defect key is released to you only after it (week 9).

Team project milestones ​

30% of the course for CS [4xxx], 25% for CS [5xxx]; 150 points: M0 15, M1 30, M2 40, Final 65. Deliverables and dates are defined in Team Project Milestones. Every milestone is graded from a snapshot of your team fork at the deadline (the commit of main, plus open branches and PRs). Rows marked Team are scored once for the team; rows marked Individual are scored for each member. Peer evaluation can adjust a member's share of the Team rows only (how).

Course documents in the team fork live in its course/ folder (why).

M0: proposal ​

15 points, all Team. End of week 5.

Final: report and repository ​

35 of the Final's 65 points, all Team. The repository snapshot is your fork's main at the start of the final exam slot, which staff tag final; the report is due on Canvas by 11:59 pm on the last day of finals week. The other 30 points are the final defense. This follows UT Austin's rule: "demonstrated behavior, reproducible checks, controlled engineering decisions, or honest limitations", not feature count (CS 378).

CriterionExemplaryProficientDevelopingMissing
Demonstrated behavior8 · A grader can build the tagged snapshot from the README in a fresh Codespace, and the contribution works as the report says6 · Builds; works with one gap the report names3 · Builds only with help, or works less than claimed0 · Doesn't build
Reproducible checks9 · One documented command or CI run reproduces the tests, mutation scores, scans and evals; every number in the report matches what it produces7 · Most numbers reproducible3 · Numbers with no way to reproduce them0 · No checks
Engineering decisions6 · Two decisions, each with the alternatives, the evidence and what you would watch for5 · Two decisions with reasons2 · Decisions stated without reasons0 · None
Honest limitations (🔴 AI off)6 · Known issues, threats to the validity of your own numbers, and what you would do next; nothing in the repository contradicts them5 · Known issues and next steps2 · "No known issues", or generic0 · Missing
Process and AI use6 · A summary of course/AI_LOG.md: what was delegated, what verification caught (with examples), what the agents couldn't do; the week 13 retrospective data; a contribution table by GitHub username (🔴 AI off)5 · Summary, data and table present2 · Thin, or the table contradicts the history0 · Missing

A team that didn't submit the week 14 draft loses 3 of these 35 points.

Final defense ​

30 of the Final's 65 points: the team demo (10, Team) and your individual questions (20, Individual). In finals week, in the scheduled 2-hour final exam slot. The format and the full rubric with level descriptors are in The Final Defense: Rubric; this is the summary.

Team demoReproducible check: one check rerun live, and its result matching the report3
Team demoHonest limitations: at least one real limitation stated without being asked3
Individual (🔴 AI off)Explain your contribution5
Individual (🔴 AI off)Defend a decision: what you specified, delegated and verified, and why5
Individual (🔴 AI off)Predict or modify: a live "what if", or how you would test a change5

Taking the final defense is required for a C or higher (letter grades).

Reading responses and Seminar participation ​

10% of the course for CS [4xxx], 5% for CS [5xxx]. The component combines reading responses (60%) and Seminar participation (40%): weight × (0.6 × responses ÷ 60 + 0.4 × participation ÷ 24).

Reading response (5 points) ​

150–250 words, 🔴 AI off, on Canvas, due before the Seminar (default 11:59 pm the night before; week 1: before the Lab). Weeks 1–15, lowest three dropped. Graded on substance, not polish or grammar. This short rubric has three levels.

The claim and its evidence2 · The reading's main claim, with at least one number or concrete finding, stated accurately1 · A summary that misses the main claim, or gets a number wrong0 · Not submitted
