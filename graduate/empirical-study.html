The Empirical Study ​

A small, real piece of research: replicate or extend a published result about AI in software engineering, at $0, with open-weight models and public data or your own artifacts, and report it in a short paper. It is the graduate version of the course's habit of checking claims instead of trusting them.

ItemDetails
WhoCS [5xxx] students, alone or in a pair
ScopeA replication or extension from the replicable list, or a proposal the instructor approves
Cost$0: the lab machine's local models, free tiers under the data rule, public datasets
PeopleNo human-subjects research without IRB approval (rules)
MilestonesProtocol (week 6) · interim results (week 11) · paper draft (week 14) · 10-minute talk (week 15) · 4–6 page paper in ACM format (finals week)
Graded as11% of the course: protocol 1.5% · interim results 1.5% · talk 2% · paper 6% (grading)
AI use🟡 AI limited: analysis code may be AI-assisted and is disclosed; the research question, the design and the prose are yours (details)

Choosing a study ​

The replicable list ​

These five studies from the course's evidence review can each be replicated or extended in about four weeks of part-time work, at $0, with open-weight models and public data (evidence notes; also on the reading list as the replicable studies). The one-line plan is a starting point; your protocol makes it precise.

StudyWhat it foundA plan at $0
Liang, Garg and Zilouchian Moghaddam, "The SWE-Bench Illusion" (arXiv 2506.12286)From issue text alone, models named the buggy file far more often on SWE-bench than on other repositories, a sign of memorizationRun the file-path probe with 2–3 open-weight models on the lab machine, on 200 SWE-bench Verified issues and 200 issues created after the models' training cutoffs (from SWE-rebench or SWE-bench-Live), and compare the two rates with bootstrap confidence intervals
Zhao, Zhou and Cohen, coverage and mutation scores of LLM-generated suites (arXiv 2607.22880)Coverage and mutation scores of LLM-written suites track real-bug detection in regression settings, but not when the code may already be buggyGenerate pytest suites with one local model for 10 BugsInPy projects (buggy and fixed versions), measure coverage.py and mutmut scores, and correlate both with whether each suite detects the real bug
Chowdhury et al., code-review agents in pull requests (arXiv 2604.03196)PRs reviewed only by review agents merged less often than human-reviewed ones, and most agents' comments had low signalRepeat the merge-rate and signal analysis on a newer slice of the AIDev dataset or GH Archive, and hand-code 100 review comments with two raters, reporting Cohen's κ. Best as a pair study: you and your partner are the raters. Work with the data in aggregate and name no individuals
Tam et al. and .txt's rebuttal, structured output and reasoning (EMNLP 2024; Say What You Mean)Tam et al. found format restrictions hurt reasoning; .txt traced that to mismatched prompts and an LLM answer parserWith two local 7–8B models and Outlines or llama.cpp grammars, cross prompt (matched or mismatched) × decoding (free or constrained) × parser (regex or LLM) on GSM8K and Last Letter
Thakur et al., "Judging the Judges" (arXiv 2406.12624)Only the largest judges approached human alignment, and high percent agreement hid large score gapsRerun the comparison on the paper's released labels with 3–4 small open-weight judges, add one new bias test (answer order or verbosity), and report κ alongside percent agreement

Your own proposal ​

You may propose a different study, using public data or your own artifacts: code you wrote, agent runs you performed, labels you made yourself. Propose it to the instructor by the end of week 5. Ideas that build on the course's labs:

  • Agent-written tests versus yours. Do tests an agent writes kill fewer mutants than tests you write for the same roomly modules, across more modules and a second model? No course has published results on mutation testing of AI-written tests, so this is an open question (course notes).
  • Does an AGENTS.md change agent behavior? Extend your week 3 A/B test to 20 or more tasks on the class codebase, with a criterion fixed in advance. An observational study found less complexity growth in repositories with a committed configuration file (Denisov-Blanch et al.); yours would be an experiment on your own runs.
  • How stable is an LLM judge? Your week 12 eval set, several judges, answer-order and prompt variations, with κ against your own labels.

A good proposal has a question with an answer you don't already know, a design that could prove you wrong, a measure that fits the question, and runs that fit the lab machine's queue.

No human-subjects research ​

Without approval from the University's Institutional Review Board (IRB), your study involves no people. In practice, that means:

Not allowed without IRB approvalAllowed
Surveys, interviews or experiments with anyone, including classmatesPublic datasets, used in aggregate and under their licenses
Studying classmates' course work: their PRs, reviews, AI_LOG.md entries, peer evaluations or grades, even where the repository is publicYour own artifacts: your code, your agent runs, your labels
Studying maintainers: submitting PRs, issues or comments as an experiment, or measuring how people respond to youModel outputs you generate

The cautionary case is real: in 2021 the Linux kernel banned contributions from the University of Minnesota after a research group submitted deliberately flawed patches to study the review process (The Register; LWN). If your idea involves people in any way, talk to the instructor before writing the protocol. IRB review takes weeks, so in a one-term study the answer is almost always to change the design. If you hope to publish your study later, say so early.

Compute and data at $0 ​

  • The lab machine serves open-weight models through Ollama to the whole class, as one queue. Plan batch runs early, run them overnight or at quiet times, and log every run.
  • Free API tiers are fine for public data only, under the course's data rule: free tiers may train on your prompts.
  • Budget your runs in your protocol: number of items × conditions × repetitions × models, and what you'll cut if it doesn't fit.
  • Spending caps at zero on anything metered, as the agent safety contract says.
  • Name exact versions. The course's materials keep model names and versions in TOOLS.md, but your paper is research: record the exact model, version or tag, quantization, context length, seed and prompt for every run, or nobody can reproduce it.

Milestones ​

Everything is submitted on Canvas. Code and data that you're allowed to share go in a public repository (your artifact), linked from each submission.

MilestoneDueWhat you submit
ProtocolBefore the week 7 SeminarThe protocol, from STUDY_PROTOCOL.md template: research question and hypotheses; what you replicate and what you change; design, materials and measures; the analysis you'll run, decided now; the run budget; threats to validity you can foresee; a timeline; and a statement that no people are involved
Interim resultsBefore the week 12 SeminarTwo pages: the pipeline running end to end, results for at least one condition with their uncertainty, what went differently from the protocol, and the revised plan
Paper draftBefore the week 15 SeminarThe complete paper as far as it can be written; feedback comes back within a week
TalkThe week 15 Seminar10 minutes plus 2–3 minutes of questions. With more than about four graduate students, talks continue into the Lab
Final paper11:59 pm on the last day of finals week4–6 pages in ACM format, plus the link to your artifact

The paper ​

  • Format: ACM's two-column conference format (the acmart LaTeX class with the sigconf option, or ACM's Word template), 4–6 pages excluding references.
  • Sections: introduction (question and why it matters); the original study and what you replicate or extend; method; results; threats to validity; related work; conclusion; an artifact statement linking your code, data (where the license allows), seeds and exact commands.
  • Report what the protocol said you would, and every deviation from it with its reason. A replication that fails to reproduce the original result is a perfectly good result, if it's done carefully.

Working in a pair ​

  • A bigger scope: more models, more conditions, both halves of an extension, or (for a study like Chowdhury et al.'s) the two independent raters that κ needs.
  • Both of you write, both of you present. In the talk, each partner presents part and answers questions.
  • A contribution statement on Canvas (🔴 AI off), agreed by both, says who did what. It stays on Canvas.
  • Normally the same score; if the record shows a large imbalance, the instructor talks with both partners and may adjust individual scores.

AI use ​

🟡 AI limited. Analysis code, data-processing scripts and plotting code may be written with AI help; disclose it in the paper's artifact statement and in your artifact's AI_LOG.md, with Assisted-by: trailers on its commits. The research question, the design, and the prose of the protocol, interim report and paper are yours (🔴 AI off). An AI may not generate, label or "clean" your data in any way you don't report as part of the method; if a model labels data, that's a condition of your study, not a shortcut. The complete course list is in the AI use policy.
