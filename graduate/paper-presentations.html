Paper Presentations ​

Each CS [5xxx] student presents one research paper to the class and serves once as discussant for a classmate's. Undergraduates take part too, with one question each. The goal is to read research the way an engineer has to now: what does it show, how strong is that, and what should change in practice?

ItemDetails
WhoEvery CS [5xxx] student: one presentation and one discussant role. Every other student posts one question beforehand
WhenOne presentation per Seminar, weeks 3–14. That Seminar's concept talk shrinks to 18 minutes, and the presentation and discussion take the reading-discussion block and more
LengthA 20-minute presentation, then 12 minutes of discussion that you lead, opened by the discussant
Graded asPresentation 4% (100-point rubric) · discussant 1% (10 points) (rubrics)
AI use🟢 AI on for preparing slides and figures (say so on your last slide); 🔴 AI off for discussant questions and posted class questions; you present and answer live

The papers ​

Choose from the course's graduate seminar set: 19 papers, plus two alternates, each suited to a 20-minute presentation (the same set as the reading list's graduate seminar set). "Fits" is the week whose topic the paper belongs to, with alternatives after "or"; the presentation goes in one of those weeks so the paper joins that week's discussion. Weeks 3, 5, 11 and 13 have few natural fits, so papers move there to balance the schedule.

To present a paper that isn't on the list, propose it to the instructor by the end of week 1, with the week it fits and why it's worth 20 minutes.

Schedule rules ​

  • Week 1: rank three papers on Canvas. End of week 2: the instructor posts the schedule of presenters and discussants.
  • One presentation per Seminar, from week 3 to week 14, in the week the paper fits. If two students want the same week, the second moves to the paper's alternative week or a neighboring one.
  • More graduate students than Seminars? Two 15-minute presentations on paired papers share a Seminar, or students present in pairs with a longer value-added part; the instructor decides in week 2.
  • Swaps between two students are fine with two weeks' notice to the instructor.
  • Discussant pairings never put you as discussant in the same week as your own presentation.
  • Recognized absences (Regulations M-1 and M-3) are rescheduled without penalty.
  • Week 15 holds the study talks, not paper presentations.

Preparing the presentation ​

A good 20 minutes does more than summarize. Combining CU Boulder's rubric with NC State's checklist (GAI4SE):

  1. The biggest takeaways first, and why the paper matters for engineers using agents now.
  2. The method, honestly: who or what was studied, what was measured and compared, and the characteristics of the data. Distinguish what the paper shows from what it claims, and tag your own claims about it observed or inferred.
  3. The results, properly: the numbers that matter, with their uncertainty, and the ones that don't add up (several papers on the list have a figure that disagrees with its own table).
  4. Value added: something the paper doesn't give the class. CU Boulder's examples are a hands-on activity or your own analysis of the tool. At $0 that could be running the paper's probe on one model on the lab machine, applying its checklist to the class codebase, or reproducing one table from released data.
  5. Discussion questions that connect it to the course: the class's own labs, the team project, the week's topic.

Send the instructor a one-paragraph outline a week before, share your slides with the discussant and the instructor 48 hours before the Seminar, and post them on Canvas afterward.

The discussant ​

10 points, 1% of the course. As discussant for a classmate's presentation you:

  1. Read the paper before you see the slides.
  2. Prepare three questions (🔴 AI off) and send them to the presenter and the instructor 48 hours before the Seminar: one about the method, one about whether the results hold up, and one about what they mean for practice or for this course.
  3. Open the discussion with one of them, in the Seminar.
  4. Submit a short note on Canvas (🔴 AI off, about 250 words) within 48 hours afterward: your three questions, what the discussion settled, and what it didn't.

Questions from the class ​

In every week with a presentation, each student who isn't presenting or discussing, undergraduate or graduate, posts one question about the paper on the Canvas discussion by 11:59 pm the night before the Seminar (🔴 AI off). Read the paper's abstract and introduction, plus at least one results section, before you write it. Cornell's CS 6158 builds its discussions the same way, from classmates' posted questions (Cornell CS 6158).

Posting your question by the deadline counts as taking part in that Seminar (participation rubric). In a week with a presentation, the paper is usually that week's reading, so your reading response and your question can draw on the same reading.
