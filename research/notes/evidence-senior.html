Evidence Base and Reading List: "Engineering Software with AI" (CS 4xxx/5xxx) ​

Research date: 2 November 2026. Scope: 2024 to November 2026, professional level. Everything below is new relative to research/notes/pedagogy-practices-risks.md, research/notes/2026-10/evidence-incidents-practices.md, resources/case-studies.md and resources/reading-list.md. Each topic's "Already collected" line points back to those files.

Format. Citation and URL, venue and date, finding with numbers, Teach in:. Labels: [PR] peer-reviewed or accepted; [Pre] preprint; [Ind-R] industry research; [Vendor]; [Docs]; [News]; [Pract]; [Legal]. [Grad seminar] (19) suits a 20-minute presentation. [Replicable] (5) can be replicated in about 4 weeks for $0 with open-weight models and public data. Numbers come from the arXiv, ACM or venue page or the primary post. Anything taken only from coverage says so.

1. Productivity and quality at professional level ​

Teaching point: better designs find smaller, less certain speed effects. Output proxies (tasks, PRs, commits) rise. Quality signals (warnings, complexity, review load) tend to get worse, and nobody measures delivered value.

  • Cui, Demirer, Jaffe, Musolff, Peng & Salz. "The Effects of Generative AI on High-Skilled Work: Evidence from Three Field Experiments with Software Developers." Management Science (2026), DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535. https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/fpi/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00535 ; https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4945566 [PR]. Randomized Copilot rollouts at Microsoft, Accenture and a Fortune 100 firm (4,867 developers) produced +26.08% completed tasks (SE 10.3%). Juniors gained 27–39% and seniors 8–13%. Adoption reached only about 60% after a year, and code quality was not observed. Teach in: Productivity. [Grad seminar]

  • Paradis et al. (Google). "How much does AI impact development speed? An enterprise-based randomized controlled trial." arXiv 2410.12944 (Oct 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12944 [Pre/Ind-R]. With 96 Google engineers, the best estimate is about 21% less time, with a wide CI. Teach in: Productivity.

  • Borg et al. "Echoes of AI: Investigating the Downstream Effects of AI Assistants on Software Maintainability." Registered report, in-principle acceptance at ICSME 2025; arXiv 2507.00788 (v3 Feb 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00788 [PR]. There were 151 participants, 95% of them professionals. AI cut median completion time 30.7% (habitual users 55.9%), but in a follow-up RCT other developers evolving that code showed no significant difference in time or quality. Its preregistered design makes it a good model of method. Teach in: Productivity; Large codebases. [Grad seminar]

  • He, Miller, Agarwal, Kästner & Vasilescu. "Does AI-Assisted Coding Deliver? A Difference-in-Differences Study of Cursor's Impact on Software Projects." MSR '26; arXiv 2511.04427. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04427 [PR]. The study compares 807 Cursor-adopting repositories with 1,380 matched controls. Lines added rose 281% in month 1, then the gain disappeared after about two months, while static-analysis warnings (+29.7%) and complexity (+40.7%) stayed high. In their model, doubling complexity goes with 64.5% fewer lines added. Note that §5.1.2 gives 25.1% for complexity, which does not match Table 2. Teach in: Productivity; CI gates. [Grad seminar]

  • Denisov-Blanch et al. "A Few Pages of Markdown: Committed AI Configuration and Lower Quality Cost after Coding-Agent Adoption." arXiv 2608.25241 (v2 Sep 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25241 [Pre]. In 441 repositories, agents raised commits 28–38%. Agent-first repositories with no committed AI configuration file saw complexity rise +53%, vs +27% in those with one. 73.8% of config files were never edited after the first commit. It links context engineering to quality, though the results are observational. Teach in: Productivity; Context engineering.

  • Murphy-Hill, Butler & Savelieva. "Adoption and Impact of Command-Line AI Coding Agents: A Study of Microsoft's Early 2026 Rollout of Claude Code and GitHub Copilot CLI." arXiv 2607.01418 (Jul 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01418 [Pre/Ind-R]. Among tens of thousands of engineers, adopters merged about 24% more PRs than their counterfactual, and the lift held for four months. Adoption spread mainly through social networks. For scale, Robbes et al. estimate that 22.2–28.7% of 128,018 GitHub projects had adopted agents ("Agentic Much?", arXiv 2601.18341, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18341 [Pre]). Teach in: Productivity; Team workflows.

  • Li, Zhang & Hassan. "The Rise of AI Teammates in Software Engineering (SE) 3.0" (the AIDev dataset). arXiv 2507.15003 (Jul 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15003 ; data https://github.com/SAILResearch/AI_Teammates_in_SE3 [Pre]. 456,000+ PRs by Codex, Devin, Copilot, Cursor and Claude Code in 61,000 repositories. Agent PRs are faster but accepted less often. It underlies most MSR 2026 agent studies. Teach in: Productivity; Code review.

  • Watanabe et al. "On the Use of Agentic Coding: An Empirical Study of Pull Requests on GitHub." arXiv 2509.14745 (v3 Feb 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14745 [Pre]. Of 567 Claude Code PRs in 157 projects, 83.8% were merged, but 45.1% of those needed further changes, mostly bug fixes, docs and project conventions. Teach in: Code review.

  • Peralta et al. "Why Are Agentic Pull Requests Merged or Rejected? An Empirical Study." MSR 2026; arXiv 2605.22534. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22534 [PR]. In 717 hand-inspected cases (from 9,799 reviewed PRs), only 35.7% of rejections were clear agent failures, 31.2% were workflow constraints and 33.1% had no visible rationale. "Rejected" is therefore a noisy label for "bad". Teach in: Code review.

  • Siddiq et al. "Security in the Age of AI Teammates: An Empirical Study of Agentic Pull Requests on GitHub." arXiv 2601.00477 (v2 Sep 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.00477 [Pre]. About 4% of 33,000+ agentic PRs (1,293) are security-related. They merge less often and wait longer for review. Teach in: Security; Code review.

Already collected: the METR 2025 RCT, its Feb 2026 update and the May 2026 survey; DORA 2025 and the DORA ROI report (2026.1); Stack Overflow 2025/2026; GitClear; CodeRabbit (pedagogy-practices-risks.md §3, §5; 2026-10/… §2). The METR paper for a seminar is Becker et al., arXiv 2507.09089, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089. A check on 2 Nov 2026 found no 2026 DORA annual report (https://dora.dev/ai/roi).

2. Benchmarks and their limits ​

Teaching point: a score combines model, scaffold, test adequacy and contamination. Every major SWE benchmark has been shown to overstate capability, and maintainers merge far fewer passing patches than graders accept.

  • Deng et al. (Scale AI). "SWE-Bench Pro: Can AI Agents Solve Long-Horizon Software Engineering Tasks?" arXiv 2509.16941 (v2 Nov 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16941 [Pre]. 1,865 tasks from 41 repositories, made contamination-resistant by using GPL repositories and startups' private code. Top scores are 43.6% on the public set and 17.8% on the commercial set. The conclusion's "about 23%" matches neither table. Teach in: Benchmarks.

  • OpenAI. "Why SWE-bench Verified no longer measures frontier coding capabilities" (23 Feb 2026). https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/ [Ind-R]. The benchmark's co-creator stopped reporting it, citing contamination and flawed tests, and recommended SWE-bench Pro. According to coverage (the page returned 403), 59.4% of 138 audited hard tasks had material test or specification flaws (https://aiweekly.co/alerts/openai-drops-swe-bench-verified-backs-pro-after-flaw-audit [News]). Teach in: Benchmarks.

  • Gabor. "SWE-Bench Pro is even worse" (24 Feb 2026). https://jonathanpgabor.substack.com/p/swe-bench-pro-is-even-worse [Pract]. A hand audit of 100 random Pro tasks found lenient tests, a test that requires a since-fixed bug, and "requirements" text that leaks the gold patch. Teach in: Benchmarks.

  • Aleithan et al. "SWE-Bench+: Enhanced Coding Benchmark for LLMs." arXiv 2410.06992 (Oct 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06992 [Pre]. In 32.67% of successful SWE-Agent+GPT-4 patches the solution was already in the issue text, and 31.08% passed only because tests were weak. With those filtered out, the resolve rate fell from 12.47% to 3.97%. Teach in: Benchmarks; Verification.

  • Wang, Pradel & Liu. "Are 'Solved Issues' in SWE-bench Really Solved Correctly? An Empirical Study." arXiv 2503.15223 (v2 Sep 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223 [Pre]. Their differential tool (PatchDiff) found 29.6% of plausible patches behave differently from the ground truth, and 28.6% of those are certainly wrong. A further 7.8% are counted as passing despite failing the full test suite. Together these inflate resolve rates by 6.2 points. Teach in: Benchmarks; Verification. [Grad seminar]

  • Liang, Garg & Zilouchian Moghaddam (Microsoft). "The SWE-Bench Illusion: When State-of-the-Art LLMs Remember Instead of Reason." arXiv 2506.12286 (v4 Dec 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12286 [Pre]. From the issue text alone, models name the buggy file up to 76% of the time on SWE-bench vs 53% elsewhere, and reproduce function 5-grams verbatim at 35% vs 18%. Teach in: Benchmarks. [Grad seminar] [Replicable] How: run the file-path probe with 2–3 open-weight models (Ollama) on 200 SWE-bench Verified issues and 200 post-cutoff SWE-rebench or SWE-bench-Live issues, and compare the two with bootstrap CIs.

  • METR. "Research Update: Algorithmic vs. Holistic Evaluation" (12 Aug 2025), https://metr.org/blog/2025-08-12-research-update-towards-reconciling-slowdown-with-time-horizons ; "Many SWE-bench-Passing PRs Would Not Be Merged into Main" (10 Mar 2026), https://metr.org/notes/2026-03-10-many-swe-bench-passing-prs-would-not-be-merged-into-main/ [Ind-R]. In 2025, none of 15 reviewed agent PRs was mergeable as-is, even though 38% passed the tests, and fixing them took 42 minutes on average. In 2026, four maintainers of scikit-learn, Sphinx and pytest reviewed 296 test-passing agent PRs. Their merge rate ran 24.2 points below the automated grader after normalizing to golden patches, and they merged only 68% of the human golden patches. Teach in: Benchmarks; Code review.

  • Newer SWE benchmarks. SWE-bench Multimodal (Yang et al., arXiv 2410.03859), https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03859 ; SWE-bench-Live (Zhang et al., arXiv 2505.23419), https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23419 ; SWE-rebench (Nebius, arXiv 2505.20411, NeurIPS 2025 per the authors), https://huggingface.co/papers/2505.20411 ; LiveCodeBench (Jain et al., arXiv 2403.07974), https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974 [Pre]. Multimodal has 617 JavaScript tasks with images, and the best system resolved 12%. Live has 1,319 post-2024 issues in 93 repositories and shows a "substantial gap" relative to SWE-bench. Rebench and LiveCodeBench refresh tasks so scores can be windowed by date against a model's cutoff. Teach in: Benchmarks.

  • Merrill et al. "Terminal-Bench: Benchmarking Agents on Hard, Realistic Tasks in Command Line Interfaces." ICLR 2026; arXiv 2601.11868. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11868 ; https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/444a3737adaee10d86ad2ef5f74468e6-Abstract-Conference.html [PR]. Terminal-Bench 2.0 has 89 terminal tasks, each with an environment, a reference solution and verification tests. Frontier agents score below 65%. Teach in: Benchmarks; How agents work.

  • Zhu et al. "Establishing Best Practices for Building Rigorous Agentic Benchmarks." arXiv 2507.02825 (v5 Aug 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02825 [Pre]. The Agentic Benchmark Checklist (ABC) shows that task or reward flaws can distort scores by up to 100% in relative terms: SWE-bench Verified has weak tests, and τ-bench counted empty responses as successes. Applying ABC cut CVE-Bench's overestimation by 33%. Teach in: Benchmarks. [Grad seminar]

  • Kwa et al. (METR). "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks." NeurIPS 2025; arXiv 2503.14499 (v4 Jul 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14499 [PR]. The paper defines the 50% time horizon, about 50 minutes for Claude 3.7 Sonnet, and finds it has been doubling roughly every 7 months since 2019. Teach in: Benchmarks; Productivity. [Grad seminar]

Five questions to ask of any benchmark claim:

  1. Which harness? Scaffolds alone shift scores by tens of points (SWE-bench Pro, Tables 1 vs 5).
  2. What does "pass" mean? Leaks and weak tests inflate scores, and passing is not mergeable (METR, 24 points).
  3. Does the task predate the model's cutoff?
  4. Is it pass^k, with a confidence interval (§6)?
  5. Does it survive the ABC checklist?

Already collected: Semgrep on SusVibes (working vs secure), 2026-10/… §2.

3. Verification: tests, mutation, properties, oracles, formal-methods-lite ​

Teaching point: LLM tests reach easy coverage and miss boundaries, and they encode what the code does rather than what it should do. Mutation score is the better gate. LLMs are good at producing mutants, and property-based testing and verification-aware languages are now within reach.

  • Alshahwan et al. (Meta). "Automated Unit Test Improvement using Large Language Models at Meta." FSE 2024; arXiv 2402.09171. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.09171 [PR/Ind-R]. Of TestGen-LLM's tests, 75% built, 57% passed reliably and 25% raised coverage (a build → pass → coverage filter), and engineers accepted 73% of its recommendations. Teach in: Verification.

  • Foster, Gulati, Harman et al. (Meta). "Mutation-Guided LLM-based Test Generation at Meta." FSE 2025 Industry; arXiv 2501.12862. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12862 [PR/Ind-R]. ACH generates mutants that represent a concern (privacy), then writes tests to kill them: 9,095 mutants and 571 tests across 10,795 classes. Its equivalent-mutant detector reached 0.95/0.96 precision/recall with preprocessing, and engineers accepted 73% of the tests. Teach in: Verification. [Grad seminar]

  • Wang, Chen, Deng, Lin, Harman, Papadakis & Zhang. "A Comprehensive Study on Large Language Models for Mutation Testing." arXiv 2406.09843 (v5 Jan 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09843 [Pre]. On 851 real Java bugs, LLM mutants detected 87.98% of faults vs 41.64% for rule-based mutants. The cost is more non-compilable (+26.6 points), duplicate and equivalent mutants. Teach in: Verification.

  • Huang, Zhang, Harman et al. "Benchmarking LLMs for Unit Test Generation from Real-World Functions" (ULT). arXiv 2508.00408 (Aug 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00408 [Pre]. On 3,909 real Python functions with no leaked tests, LLM tests averaged 45.1% statement coverage, 30.2% branch coverage and a 40.2% mutation score, against about 92% coverage on TestEval. Teach in: Verification; Benchmarks.

  • Zhao, Zhou & Cohen. "Do Coverage and Mutation Scores of LLM-Generated Test Suites Correlate with Their Effectiveness? (Replicability Study)." ISSTA 2026 (PACMSE 3, ISSTA002); arXiv 2607.22880. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22880 [PR]. This replicates Inozemtseva & Holmes and Papadakis et al. on LLM-generated suites, with results that diverge substantially. Coverage and mutation help in regression settings but are unreliable when the code may already be buggy, which is the typical case for AI-written code. Teach in: Verification. [Grad seminar] [Replicable] How: generate pytest suites with one local model for 10 BugsInPy projects (buggy and fixed versions), measure coverage.py and mutmut scores, and correlate both with real-bug detection.

  • Shi, Luo, Yang & Tian. "Beyond Coverage and Mutation: A Blind-Spot Analysis of LLM-Generated Unit Tests." arXiv 2610.32548 (23 Oct 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32548 [Pre]. In 14,320 tests from four LLMs, boundary inputs were rarely tested: null 34.8%, negative 23.8%, empty 38.8%, vs over 93% for booleans. 15.6% of failures were wrong assertions. Teach in: Verification.

  • Konstantinou, Degiovanni & Papadakis. "Can we find bugs using LLM-generated oracles?" arXiv 2410.21136 (v2 Oct 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21136 [Pre]. Across 24 Java repositories, LLM-generated oracles capture actual rather than expected behaviour, which hides bugs, though they still beat EvoSuite's oracles. This is the mechanism behind the same group's "coding before testing" result. Teach in: Verification; Spec-driven development.

  • Maaz, DeVoe, Hatfield-Dodds & Carlini. "Agentic Property-Based Testing: Finding Bugs Across the Python Ecosystem." arXiv 2510.09907; listed at NeurIPS 2025 (https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/131710). https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09907 ; code https://github.com/mmaaz-git/agentic-pbt [PR]. A Claude Code agent, driven by one Markdown prompt, wrote and triaged Hypothesis tests for 100 packages. 56% of its reports were valid bugs, and 86% of the top-ranked 21. Three patches were merged, including one in NumPy. Teach in: Verification; Skills/prompts as tools. [Grad seminar]

  • Bursuc et al. "A benchmark for vericoding: formally verified program synthesis." arXiv 2509.22908 (Sep 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22908 [Pre]; with Kleppmann, "Prediction: AI will make formal verification go mainstream" (8 Dec 2025), https://martin.kleppmann.com/2025/12/08/ai-formal-verification.html [Pract]. Across 12,504 specifications, LLMs produced verified code for 82% in Dafny, 44% in Verus and 27% in Lean, and pure Dafny verification rose 68% → 96% in a year. Kleppmann argues the bottleneck moves to writing specifications, which links this to the spec-driven unit. Teach in: Verification (formal-methods-lite).

Already collected: Konstantinou, Tambon & Papadakis, "On the risk of coding before testing" (14% vs 25%), 2026-10/… §2 and the reading list. Willison's red/green TDD, pedagogy-practices-risks.md §4.

4. Code review at scale, CI gates and static analysis ​

Teaching point: deployed AI reviewers lead to code changes less often than humans (39–74% of comments addressed), and their effect on cycle time can go either way. Signal-to-noise is the design problem.

  • Cihan et al. "Automated Code Review In Practice." ICSE 2025 SEIP; arXiv 2412.18531. https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18531 [PR]. At Beko, Qodo PR-Agent reviewed 1,568 of 4,335 PRs. 73.8% of AI comments were resolved, but average closure time rose from 5h52m to 8h20m (+42%). Developers saw minor quality gains and some irrelevant comments. Teach in: Code review. [Grad seminar]

  • Tantithamthavorn et al. (Atlassian). "RovoDev Code Reviewer: A Large-Scale Online Evaluation of LLM-based Code Review Automation at Atlassian." ICSE 2026 SEIP; arXiv 2601.01129. https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01129v2 [PR/Ind-R]. Over a year, with 54,000+ comments in 1,900+ repositories, 38.7% of AI comments led to code changes vs 44.45% of human comments. PR cycle time fell 30.8% and human comments fell 35.6%. Read it alongside Cihan for the opposite cycle-time result. Teach in: Code review. [Grad seminar]

  • Sun et al. (ByteDance). "BitsAI-CR: Automated Code Review via LLM in Practice." arXiv 2501.15134, https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15134 [Pre/Ind-R]; and Sun, Kuang, Baltes, … Treude, "Does AI Code Review Lead to Code Changes? A Case Study of GitHub Actions," arXiv 2508.18771 (v2 Apr 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18771 [Pre]. BitsAI-CR's generate-then-filter design reached 75.0% precision and introduced an "Outdated Rate" (26.7% for Go) as a usefulness metric. The GitHub Actions study (22,000+ comments, 16 tools, 178 repositories) finds that comments which are concise, include code snippets or are manually triggered are more likely to lead to changes. Teach in: Code review; CI gates.

  • Chowdhury, Banik, Ferdous & Shamim. "From Industry Claims to Empirical Reality: An Empirical Study of Code Review Agents in Pull Requests." MSR 2026; arXiv 2604.03196. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03196 [PR]. PRs reviewed only by code-review agents merged 45.2% of the time vs 68.4% for human-reviewed PRs, and were abandoned more often. 12 of 13 agents had average signal ratios below 60%. This tests the industry claim that agents can handle 80% of PRs alone. Teach in: Code review. [Replicable] How: repeat the merge-rate and signal analysis on a newer AIDev or GH Archive slice, hand-code 100 comments with two raters, and report κ.

  • GitHub. "60 million Copilot code reviews and counting" (5 Mar 2026). https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/60-million-copilot-code-reviews-and-counting/ [Vendor]. Copilot does more than one in five reviews on GitHub. 71% surface actionable feedback and 29% deliberately say nothing. A stronger model gave +6% positive feedback for +16% latency, a concrete cost and latency trade-off. Teach in: Code review; LLM features.

  • Li, Dutta & Naik. "IRIS: LLM-Assisted Static Analysis for Detecting Security Vulnerabilities." arXiv 2405.17238 (v3 Apr 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.17238 [Pre]. An LLM infers taint specifications for CodeQL. On 120 Java CVEs, CodeQL found 27 and IRIS with GPT-4 found 55, plus 4 new vulnerabilities. Here the LLM feeds a sound analyzer instead of replacing it. Teach in: Static analysis; Security.

Already collected: CodeRabbit (1.7× issues in AI PRs), pedagogy-practices-risks.md §5; "Comment and Control" (injection via PR text against review Actions), 2026-10/… §3D. For agent-PR review, see §1 (Watanabe, Peralta) and METR in §2.

5. Security of AI-assisted development ​

Teaching point: code that works is still insecure about half the time. Agents that read untrusted text and hold tools are exploitable by design, and the defences that hold are architectural.

  • Vero et al. (ETH/LogicStar). "BaxBench: Can LLMs Generate Correct and Secure Backends?" arXiv 2502.11844 (v3 May 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11844 [Pre]. Across 392 backend tasks checked with functional tests and end-to-end exploits, the best model (o1) was 62% correct, and about half of correct programs were exploitable. Teach in: Security; Verification. [Grad seminar]

  • Liu, Zhao, Lyu, Zhang, Wang & Lo. "'Your AI, My Shell': Demystifying Prompt Injection Attacks on Agentic AI Coding Editors." arXiv 2509.22040 (v2 Apr 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22040 [Pre]. AIShellJack's 314 payloads (70 MITRE ATT&CK techniques), planted in project files and rules, made Cursor and Copilot run malicious commands up to 84% of the time. Teach in: Security. [Grad seminar]

  • Invariant Labs. "MCP Security Notification: Tool Poisoning Attacks" (1 Apr 2025), https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-security-notification ; "GitHub MCP Exploited" (26 May 2025), https://invariantlabs.ai/blog/mcp-github-vulnerability [Vendor research]. Hidden instructions in tool descriptions (seen by the model, not the user) got an agent to read SSH keys, and a server can change a description after approval. A malicious public issue led an agent with a broad token to copy private-repo data into a public PR. Invariant calls this architectural, not a server bug. Teach in: Security (MCP).

  • Willison, on General Analysis. "Supabase MCP can leak your entire SQL database" (6 Jul 2025). https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jul/6/supabase-mcp-lethal-trifecta/ [Pract]. An agent holding the RLS-bypassing service_role key reads instructions in a support ticket and copies secrets into a reply the attacker can see. One server supplies all three legs of the lethal trifecta, and making it read-only removes the exfiltration path. Teach in: Security; Agent permissions.

  • Copilot prompt-injection CVEs (2025). CVE-2025-53773, https://cybersecuritynews.com/github-copilot-rce-vulnerability/ [News]; CamoLeak, CVE-2025-59145, https://legitsecurity.com/blog/camoleak-critical-github-copilot-vulnerability-leaks-private-source-code [Vendor]. In the first, injected text made the agent set "chat.tools.autoApprove": true in .vscode/settings.json, switching itself to auto-approve mode (CVSS 7.8; patched Aug 2025). In CamoLeak, hidden PR comments made Copilot Chat leak private code as sequences of pre-signed Camo image URLs (CVSS 9.6), and GitHub responded by disabling image rendering in Copilot Chat. The lesson: agents must not be able to edit their own permissions or reach the network. Teach in: Security; Agent permissions.

  • CISA. "Widespread Supply Chain Compromise Impacting npm Ecosystem" (23 Sep 2025). https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2025/09/23/widespread-supply-chain-compromise-impacting-npm-ecosystem [Gov]. The self-replicating Shai-Hulud worm compromised 500+ packages. CISA's advice: audit lockfiles, pin to releases before 16 Sep 2025, rotate credentials and require phishing-resistant MFA. It is the background to the 2026 AI-key stealers. Teach in: Security (supply chain).

  • Debenedetti, Shumailov, … Carlini, … Tramèr (Google DeepMind/ETH). "Defeating Prompt Injections by Design" (CaMeL). arXiv 2503.18813 (v2 Jun 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18813 [Pre/Ind-R]. A privileged LLM writes the plan as code, a quarantined LLM parses untrusted data, and capability and data-flow policies gate the tools. It solves 77% of AgentDojo tasks with provable security vs 84% undefended. Teach in: Security; LLM features. [Grad seminar]

  • Beurer-Kellner et al. "Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents against Prompt Injections." arXiv 2506.08837 (Jun 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.08837 [Pre]. Six patterns with provable resistance: action-selector, plan-then-execute, LLM map-reduce, dual LLM, code-then-execute and context-minimization. Each comes with a utility and security analysis and case studies. Teach in: Security; LLM features. [Grad seminar]

  • Anthropic Engineering. "Beyond permission prompts: making Claude Code more secure and autonomous" (20 Oct 2025). https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/claude-code-sandboxing [Vendor/Docs]. Both filesystem isolation and network isolation are needed: without either one, a compromised agent can escape or exfiltrate SSH keys. Anthropic reports 84% fewer permission prompts internally, which bears on approval fatigue. Teach in: Sandboxing.

  • OWASP GenAI Security Project. "OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 2026" (4 Aug 2026). https://genai.owasp.org/resource/owasp-genai-llm-top-10-2026/ ; https://github.com/GenAI-Security-Project/GenAI-LLM-Top10 [Docs]. The new order: Prompt Injection, Sensitive Information Disclosure, Excessive Agency (up from 6th), Supply Chain, then Poisoning, Unbounded Consumption, Misinformation, Hidden Context Exposure (renamed from System Prompt Leakage), Vector/Embedding Weaknesses, and Improper Output Handling (now 10th). Use it alongside the Dec 2025 Agentic Top 10. Teach in: Security; LLM features.

Already collected: Veracode 2025/2026; Spracklen et al. (USENIX Security 2025) and arXiv 2605.17062 on package hallucination; Nx s1ngularity; Amazon Q; Replit and PocketOS deletions; the rules-file backdoor; Overeager agents; UpGuard's config study; GitSpawn; Copilot CLI context injection; SusVibes; Deng et al.; GitGuardian 2026; the OWASP Agentic Top 10 (case-studies.md 4, 7–10, 13–16; 2026-10/… §3–4).

6. Building and evaluating LLM-powered features ​

Teaching point: an eval is an experiment, so give it error bars and repeated trials. A judge is a model of a human and has to be validated against labels. Criteria shift once you look at outputs.

  • Shankar, Zamfirescu-Pereira, Hartmann, Parameswaran & Arawjo. "Who Validates the Validators? Aligning LLM-Assisted Evaluation of LLM Outputs with Human Preferences." UIST 2024, DOI 10.1145/3654777.3676450. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12272 [PR]. EvalGen keeps the generated assertions and judge prompts that agree with a user's grades. The paper names criteria drift: grading outputs changes the criteria, and some criteria only appear after seeing outputs. Don't fix a rubric before looking at data. Teach in: LLM features (evals). [Grad seminar]

  • Thakur, Choudhary, Ramayapally, Vaidyanathan & Hupkes. "Judging the Judges: Evaluating Alignment and Vulnerabilities in LLMs-as-Judges." GEM² at ACL 2025, pp. 404–430; arXiv 2406.12624. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12624 [PR]. Among 13 judges, only the largest approached human alignment, and even they deviated by up to 5 points. High percent agreement hid large score gaps, so report κ-type metrics. Teach in: LLM-as-judge pitfalls. [Replicable] How: rerun the comparison on the paper's released labels with 3–4 small open-weight judges, add one new bias test (answer order or verbosity), and report κ alongside percent agreement.

  • Wang, Guo, Gao, Fan, Chong & Xia. "Can LLMs Replace Human Evaluators? An Empirical Study of LLM-as-a-Judge in Software Engineering." ISSTA 2025; arXiv 2502.06193. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06193 [PR]. For code, the best judges correlated with humans at r ≈ 0.81 (translation) and 0.69 (generation), vs 0.34 and 0.65 for ChrF++. They beat n-gram metrics but do not equal humans. Teach in: LLM features.

  • Husain. "Using LLM-as-a-Judge For Evaluation: A Complete Guide" (29 Oct 2024, revised 2026). https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/ [Pract]. One domain expert gives binary pass/fail grades with critiques, and the judge is built iteratively until it agrees with that expert. Plan on about 100 labels per failure mode, and report true-positive and true-negative rates, because an always-"pass" judge scores 95% agreement when 5% of outputs fail. Teach in: LLM features.

  • Anthropic Engineering. "Demystifying evals for AI agents" (9 Jan 2026), https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents [Vendor/Pract]; and Miller, "Adding Error Bars to Evals," arXiv 2411.00640, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640 [Pre/Ind-R]. Anthropic distinguishes capability from regression evals and recommends grading outcomes, not step sequences, and optimizing pass^k (read from excerpts). Miller treats eval questions as a sample and gives formulas for standard errors, paired model comparisons and power analysis. Teach in: LLM features; Benchmarks.

  • Tam et al. "Let Me Speak Freely? A Study on the Impact of Format Restrictions on Performance of Large Language Models." EMNLP 2024 Industry, pp. 1218–1236, https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-industry.91/ [PR]; Kurt (.txt), "Say What You Mean," https://blog.dottxt.ai/say-what-you-mean.html [Vendor]; OpenAI, "Introducing Structured Outputs in the API" (6 Aug 2024), https://openai.com/index/introducing-structured-outputs-in-the-api/ [Vendor]. OpenAI reports 100% schema adherence vs under 40% for gpt-4-0613. Tam et al. report reasoning declines under format restrictions, but .txt traces this to mismatched prompts and an LLM answer parser. With matched prompts on Llama-3-8B, .txt finds structured output equal or better (GSM8K 0.77→0.78; Last Letter 0.73→0.77). Teach in: Structured output. [Replicable] How: with 2 local 7–8B models and Outlines or llama.cpp grammars, cross prompt (matched/mismatched) × decoding (free/constrained) × parser (regex/LLM) on GSM8K and Last Letter.

  • Yao, Shinn, Razavi & Narasimhan. "τ-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains." arXiv 2406.12045. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045 [Pre]. Tool-calling agents succeeded on under 50% of tasks, and pass^8 was below 25% in retail. It introduced the pass^k reliability metric. Teach in: Tool calling; Benchmarks.

  • Barnett et al. "Seven Failure Points When Engineering a Retrieval Augmented Generation System." CAIN 2024, DOI 10.1145/3644815.3644945, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05856 [PR]; with Es et al., "Ragas," arXiv 2309.15217, https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15217 [Pre]. The failure points: missing content, missed top-ranked documents, lost in consolidation, not extracted, wrong format, wrong specificity, incomplete. The lessons are that RAG can only be fully validated in operation and that robustness evolves rather than being designed in. Ragas metrics (context relevance, faithfulness, answer quality) are themselves LLM judges, so validate them the same way. Teach in: RAG.

  • Hong, Troynikov & Huber (Chroma). "Context Rot: How Increasing Input Tokens Impacts LLM Performance" (14 Jul 2025). https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot [Vendor research]. In all 18 models, performance fell as input length grew, and faster with distractors. On LongMemEval, a focused prompt of about 300 tokens beat the full 113k-token one. It is the cost and latency case for context engineering. Teach in: Cost/latency; Context engineering.

Already collected: Anthropic, "Effective context engineering for AI agents," reading-list.md week 6.

7. Skills and learning (professionals and advanced students) ​

Teaching point: evidence of skill loss now goes beyond novices, and the early-career job market is the structural version of the same worry.

  • Lee, Sarkar, Tankelevitch, Drosos, Rintel, Banks & Wilson (Microsoft Research). "The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking: Self-Reported Reductions in Cognitive Effort and Confidence Effects From a Survey of Knowledge Workers." CHI 2025, DOI 10.1145/3706598.3713778. https://advait.org/files/lee_2025_ai_critical_thinking_survey.pdf [PR]. Among 319 knowledge workers (936 examples), higher confidence in the AI predicted less critical thinking, and higher self-confidence predicted more. Effort shifted toward verifying and integrating AI output. Teach in: Skills. [Grad seminar]

  • Balepur, Baumler, Chen, Choi, Rudinger & Boyd-Graber. "(Im)Paired Programming: Coding Agents Improve Productivity but Harm Understanding." arXiv 2607.26375 (Jul 2026, in progress). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26375 [Pre]. 54 students building a website finished faster with an editing agent but understood less and extended their code worse without AI. Auto-accepting went with lower comprehension, yet they still preferred agents. The abstract gives no effect sizes. Teach in: Skills; How agents work.

  • Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen. "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" (Stanford Digital Economy Lab, Aug 2025) and follow-up note (9 Feb 2026). https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canaries-interest-rates-and-timinga-more-on-recent-drivers-of-employment-changes-for-young-workers ; https://leaddev.com/hiring/young-devs-steepest-losses-exposed-roles-stanford-study-finds [Pre/News]. ADP payroll data show employment of developers aged 22–25 down nearly 20% from the late-2022 peak, with about a 16% relative decline in exposed jobs after controls. The 2026 note finds the decline significant only after 2024 under stricter controls, partly driven by non-AI factors. Teach in: Skills; Professional responsibility.

  • Yu & Moon. "Who Will Become the Next Senior? How Generative AI Erodes the Development Pathway in Software Engineering." AIES 2026; arXiv 2607.17067. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17067 [PR]; and Baltes, Cheong & Treude, "'An Endless Stream of AI Slop'," arXiv 2603.27249, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27249 [Pre]. From 14 interviews, Yu & Moon describe "Absorption": entry-level work folds into senior-plus-AI workflows, and the productive struggle that builds expertise disappears. Baltes et al. code 1,154 Reddit and HN posts into review friction, quality degradation, and forces and consequences, including collective deskilling. Teach in: Skills; OSS norms.

Already collected: Shen & Tamkin's skill-formation RCT (arXiv 2601.20245; quiz scores 17% lower), a strong seminar alternate; Bergh et al.; Sankaranarayanan; Gardella et al.; Aribe & Labastida; Ahmad on comprehension debt (pedagogy-practices-risks.md §6; 2026-10/… §1).

8. Open-source and professional norms: policies, licensing, attribution ​

Teaching point: projects converge on disclose, understand, take responsibility, enforced by a human sign-off. They disagree on how to record AI involvement, and the policies change quickly.

  • Gentoo Council "AI policy" (14 Apr 2024), https://wiki.gentoo.org/wiki/Project:Council/AI_policy ; NetBSD "Commit guidelines" (May 2024), https://www.netbsd.org/developers/commit-guidelines.html [Docs]. Gentoo bans content created with NLP AI tools on copyright, quality and ethics grounds, and enforces it by trust. NetBSD treats LLM output as "presumed to be tainted code" that needs prior written approval by core. Debian declined to adopt a similar ban (https://itwire.com/business-it-news/open-source/debian-project-not-keen-on-drafting-policy-to-cover-ai-contributions [News]). Teach in: OSS norms; Licensing.

  • QEMU. "Code provenance" (current), https://www.qemu.org/docs/master/devel/code-provenance.html ; Bonzini, "[PATCH v2] docs/devel: relax policy on AI-generated contributions" (29 May 2026), https://patchew.org/QEMU/20260529094619.1034458-1-pbonzini@redhat.com/ [Docs]. As of 2 Nov 2026 the docs still decline any contribution believed to include AI-generated content, though AI for research or debugging is allowed. The pending proposal would allow mechanical changes, bug fixes of about 20 lines, docs and tests, recorded with an AI-used-for: trailer (explicitly not Assisted-by). It drew acks and strong objections, and no decision is recorded. Teach in: OSS norms; Attribution.

  • Linux kernel. Documentation/process/coding-assistants.rst, https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/master/Documentation/process/coding-assistants.rst [Docs]; Posch, "Linux Kernel Drops AI Tag Requirement, Uses Meta AI For Code Reviews," Hackaday (15 Oct 2026), https://hackaday.com/2026/10/15/linux-kernel-drops-ai-tag-requirement-uses-meta-ai-for-code-reviews/ [News]. Code must be GPL-2.0-only compatible, "AI agents MUST NOT add Signed-off-by tags," and the human takes full responsibility. The master copy no longer mentions Assisted-by: per Hackaday, maintainers dropped the tag requirement in Oct 2026, and networking maintainers secured budget to run several frontier models on each patch for review. Teach in: OSS norms; Code review.

  • Ghostty AI_POLICY.md, https://github.com/ghostty-org/ghostty/blob/main/AI_POLICY.md ; Django, "AI-assisted contributions," https://docs.djangoproject.com/en/dev/internals/contributing/writing-code/submitting-patches/ [Docs]. Ghostty: "All AI usage in any form must be disclosed." Contributors must be able to explain changes without AI, AI media is banned, and people who submit slop may go on a public denouncement list. Django requires disclosing the tools and what they did and verifying output against the full test suite. It includes a "Note for AI Tools" addressed to agents, and asks contributors not to request automated AI reviews on PRs because they "often generate noise". Teach in: OSS norms; Attribution.

  • curl ends its bug bounty (31 Jan 2026). https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/21/curl_ends_bug_bounty/ ; https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/curl-ending-bug-bounty-program-after-flood-of-ai-slop-reports/ [News]. Stenberg ended the HackerOne bounty (up to $10,000) to remove the incentive for AI-generated junk reports. Teach in: OSS norms; Security.

  • Thaler v. Perlmutter, cert. denied, No. 25-449 (U.S. 2 Mar 2026), https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/case-files/thaler-v-perlmutter ; https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/supreme-court-denies-review-in-ai-2865518/ [Legal]; and Bartz v. Anthropic (N.D. Cal.), https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/07/judge-approves-record-1-5-billion-settlement-involving-anthropic/ [News]. The human-authorship requirement stands: the denial is not a merits ruling, and how far AI-assisted works are protected is still open. This confirms what the October notes had left unverified. In Bartz, training on lawfully bought books was held to be fair use but keeping 7M+ pirated books was not. The case settled for $1.5B (about $3,000 per work), the leading example of training-data provenance. Teach in: Licensing & provenance.

  • Xu, Gao, He & Zhou. "LiCoEval: Evaluating LLMs on License Compliance in Code Generation." ICSE 2025; arXiv 2408.02487. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02487 [PR]. Across 14 LLMs, even the best produced 0.88–2.01% of outputs "strikingly similar" to existing OSS code, and most gave inaccurate licence information, especially for copyleft. It puts a number on the "tainted code" argument. Teach in: Licensing & attribution. [Grad seminar]

  • Horikawa et al. "Agent or Human? Detecting Commits by AI Coding Agents in the Absence of Authorship Signatures." arXiv 2610.36229 (26 Oct 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36229 [Pre]. Across 7.8M commits in 1,226 repositories, detectors with trailers masked reach AUC up to 0.859, but at realistic prevalence precision is only 0.332, and they transfer poorly to new agents. Detection "cannot replace recorded attribution," so trailer counts are a lower bound. This is the empirical case for disclosure rules. Teach in: Attribution.

GitHub; the Copyright Office Part 2 report (2026-10/… §5; reading-list.md week 8). Correction needed: the October notes describe the kernel's Assisted-by: AGENT_NAME:MODEL_VERSION tag as current. As of Nov 2026 it is gone from the master policy.
