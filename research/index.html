How to read the sources ​

The notes label every item by kind, so you can weigh it:

  • [PR] peer-reviewed or accepted · [Pre] preprint · [Ind-R] industry research · [Vendor] a company writing about its own product or market · [Docs] official documentation · [News] press coverage · [Pract] practitioner writing · [Legal] court records and legal coverage.
  • In the tool notes: [official] the vendor's own page · [3rd] a third party · [snippet] seen only in a search summary · [GH] read from the GitHub API or a package registry · [A] carried over from the sibling course's notes without re-checking · UNVERIFIED could not be confirmed.
  • "Search summary" and "coverage" mean the course didn't read the primary source; numbers from them are flagged where they're used.

Dates and the two-layer rule ​

Research files are dated records: they describe what was true when they were written, so they may name models, prices and the dates of tool changes. The rest of the course keeps those facts in Tool Handout only, so that a product change means editing one file. When a fact here goes stale, don't edit the old note: add a new dated note, update TOOLS.md, and update the report or rationale if a decision changes.

Relationship to the sibling course ​

The lower-division course, Building Apps with AI, has its own research reports (September and October 2026). These notes cover what is new or different for seniors and graduate students; where they rely on the sibling's findings (some incidents, the Safe Loop, some 2025–26 studies), they say so, and the course's case studies mark those items [A].

Contributing ​

Found a newer study, a course that published outcome data, or a tool change? Open an erratum or tool change issue with the source. The gaps the course most wants filled are listed in the report's gaps section.
