Reading List ​

The evidence behind the course, organized by week. Each week's README names the one required reading (with its 150–250-word reading response on Canvas, 🔴 AI off); this page suggests a core pick per week and the rest of the evidence for going further. Tags: [Grad seminar] suits a 20-minute CS [5xxx] paper presentation (graduate track) · [Replicable] can be replicated in about four weeks at $0 with open-weight models and public data (empirical study) · [PR] peer-reviewed or accepted · [Pre] preprint · [Ind] industry research · [Docs], [News], [Pract] (practitioner) as in the research notes.

Numbers below come from the arXiv, ACM or venue page or the primary post, as recorded in the course's evidence notes (checked November 2026). Preprints are preprints: say so when you cite one. When a link breaks, search for the title and tell us.

TIP

Reading a paper for a reading response: abstract, then the figures and tables, then the threats-to-validity section, then decide whether the middle is worth your time. Your response should say what the evidence shows, what it doesn't, and what it changes about how you'd work, with one number from the paper.

Week 1 — From Vibe Coding to Agentic Engineering ​

Core pick: METR, "Many SWE-bench-Passing PRs Would Not Be Merged into Main" (Mar 2026). Short, and the course's "maintainers vs graders" teaser.

ReadWhat it showsTags
METR (above)Four maintainers of scikit-learn, Sphinx and pytest reviewed 296 test-passing agent PRs; their merge rate ran 24.2 points below the automated grader, and they merged only 68% of the human golden patches[Ind]
METR, "Algorithmic vs. Holistic Evaluation" (Aug 2025)None of 15 reviewed agent PRs was mergeable as-is, though 38% passed the tests; fixing them took 42 minutes on average[Ind]
Kwa et al., "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks", NeurIPS 2025Defines the 50% time horizon; it has doubled roughly every 7 months since 2019[PR] [Grad seminar]
Thorsten Ball, "How to build an agent"A working coding agent in under 400 lines with three tools: the shape of this week's lab[Pract]
mini-swe-agentAbout 100 lines of Python, bash only, and a strong SWE-bench Verified score: how little harness a capable model needs[Pract]

Week 2 — Inside Real Agents ​

Core pick: Anthropic Engineering, "Beyond permission prompts: making Claude Code more secure and autonomous" (Oct 2025). How a production harness thinks about permissions and sandboxes.

ReadWhat it showsTags
Anthropic (above)Filesystem and network isolation are both needed; sandboxing cut internal permission prompts by 84%[Vendor]
Watanabe et al., "On the Use of Agentic Coding"Of 567 Claude Code PRs in 157 projects, 83.8% were merged, but 45.1% of those needed further changes[Pre]
Li, Zhang & Hassan, "The Rise of AI Teammates in SE 3.0" (the AIDev dataset; data)456,000+ PRs by five agents in 61,000 repositories; agent PRs are faster but accepted less often. The dataset behind most 2026 agent-PR studies[Pre]
Murphy-Hill, Butler & Savelieva, "Adoption and Impact of Command-Line AI Coding Agents"At Microsoft, adopters merged about 24% more PRs than their counterfactual for four months; adoption spread through social networks[Pre/Ind]
Merrill et al., "Terminal-Bench", ICLR 202689 terminal tasks with environments and verification tests; frontier agents score below 65%[PR]

Week 3 — Context Engineering ​

Core pick: Denisov-Blanch et al., "A Few Pages of Markdown" (Sep 2026).

ReadWhat it showsTags
Denisov-Blanch et al. (above)In 441 repositories, agents raised commits 28–38%; agent-first repositories without a committed AI config saw complexity rise 53% vs 27% with one; 73.8% of config files were never edited after the first commit. Observational[Pre]
Hong, Troynikov & Huber (Chroma), "Context Rot"All 18 models degraded with longer inputs; a focused ~300-token prompt beat the full 113K-token one[Vendor research]
Anthropic, "Effective context engineering for AI agents"Why long sessions degrade and how harnesses manage context (collected by the sibling course)[Vendor]
Michigan EECS 498, the Elephant-Goldfish ModelWhere the Goldfish test comes from[Pract]

Week 4 — Securing Agents ​

Core pick: Simon Willison, "Supabase MCP can leak your entire SQL database" (Jul 2025). The lethal trifecta in one page.

ReadWhat it showsTags
Willison (above)One MCP server supplies all three legs; a read-only server removes the way out (case A1)[Pract]
Liu et al., "'Your AI, My Shell'"314 payloads in project files and rules made Cursor and Copilot run malicious commands up to 84% of the time[Pre] [Grad seminar]
Debenedetti et al., "Defeating Prompt Injections by Design" (CaMeL)Privileged planner, quarantined parser, capability policies: 77% of AgentDojo tasks with provable security vs 84% undefended[Pre/Ind] [Grad seminar]
Beurer-Kellner et al., "Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents against Prompt Injections"Six patterns with provable resistance, each with its utility cost[Pre] [Grad seminar]
Invariant Labs, tool poisoning and GitHub MCP exploitedHidden instructions in tool descriptions; a public issue leaking a private repository[Vendor research]
MCP Security Best PracticesThe specification's own attack catalogue[Docs]
OWASP, GenAI LLM Top 10 2026Prompt Injection first, Excessive Agency up to third[Docs]

Week 5 — Spec-Driven Development ​

Core pick: Martin Kleppmann, "Prediction: AI will make formal verification go mainstream" (Dec 2025). The bottleneck moves to writing specifications.

ReadWhat it showsTags
Kleppmann (above), with Bursuc et al., "A benchmark for vericoding"LLMs produced verified code for 82% of specifications in Dafny, 44% in Verus, 27% in Lean; pure Dafny rose from 68% to 96% in a year[Pract] / [Pre]
Konstantinou, Tambon & Papadakis, "On the risk of coding before testing"Tests generated after faulty code caught 14% of faults vs 25% when generated independently (collected by the sibling course)[Pre]
UT Austin CS 378, course pageHomework "in beats": "the spec changes under you"[Docs]

Week 6 — Tests as Oracles ​

Core pick: Maaz, DeVoe, Hatfield-Dodds & Carlini, "Agentic Property-Based Testing" (NeurIPS 2025; code).

ReadWhat it showsTags
Maaz et al. (above)An agent driven by one Markdown prompt wrote Hypothesis tests for 100 packages; 56% of its reports were valid bugs (86% of the top 21); patches merged in NumPy and elsewhere[PR] [Grad seminar]
Konstantinou, Degiovanni & Papadakis, "Can we find bugs using LLM-generated oracles?"LLM oracles capture actual rather than expected behavior, hiding bugs[Pre]
Shi, Luo, Yang & Tian, "Beyond Coverage and Mutation"In 14,320 LLM tests, null inputs were tested 34.8% of the time, negatives 23.8%, empties 38.8%, booleans over 93%[Pre]
Alshahwan et al. (Meta), "Automated Unit Test Improvement using LLMs at Meta", FSE 2024A build → pass → coverage filter: 75% built, 57% passed reliably, 25% raised coverage[PR/Ind]
Huang, Zhang, Harman et al., "Benchmarking LLMs for Unit Test Generation from Real-World Functions"On 3,909 real functions: 45.1% statement coverage, 40.2% mutation score, against about 92% coverage on an older benchmark[Pre]

Week 7 — Measuring Test Adequacy ​

Core pick: Foster, Gulati, Harman et al. (Meta), "Mutation-Guided LLM-based Test Generation at Meta" (FSE 2025 Industry).

ReadWhat it showsTags
Foster et al. (above)Mutants representing a concern (privacy), then tests to kill them; the equivalent-mutant detector reached 0.95/0.96 precision/recall; engineers accepted 73% of tests[PR/Ind] [Grad seminar]
Zhao, Zhou & Cohen, "Do Coverage and Mutation Scores of LLM-Generated Test Suites Correlate with Their Effectiveness?", ISSTA 2026The ISSTA caveat: both measures help in regression settings but are unreliable when the code may already be buggy[PR] [Grad seminar] [Replicable]
Wang, Chen, Deng et al., "A Comprehensive Study on LLMs for Mutation Testing"LLM mutants detected 87.98% of 851 real faults vs 41.64% for rule-based ones, at the cost of more non-compilable and equivalent mutants[Pre]
MutGen, arXiv 2506.02954Suites with 100% coverage can have a 4% mutation score[Pre]

Week 8 — Legacy Code ​

Core pick: He, Miller, Agarwal, Kästner & Vasilescu, "Does AI-Assisted Coding Deliver?" (MSR 2026).

ReadWhat it showsTags
He et al. (above)807 Cursor-adopting repositories vs 1,380 matched controls: lines added rose 281% in month 1 and the gain faded within about two months, while static-analysis warnings (+29.7%) and complexity (+40.7%; the text says 25.1%) stayed up[PR] [Grad seminar]
Borg et al., "Echoes of AI", ICSME 2025 registered reportAI cut median completion time 30.7%, but other developers later evolving that code showed no significant difference in time or quality. A model preregistered design[PR] [Grad seminar]
CMU 17-313, P1B refactoringAn onboarding assignment that starts with a measurable refactor on a real agent codebase[Docs]

Week 9 — Review at Scale ​

Core pick: Chowdhury, Banik, Ferdous & Shamim, "From Industry Claims to Empirical Reality" (MSR 2026).

ReadWhat it showsTags
Chowdhury et al. (above)PRs reviewed only by code-review agents merged 45.2% of the time vs 68.4% with humans; 12 of 13 agents had signal ratios below 60%[PR] [Replicable]
Cihan et al., "Automated Code Review In Practice", ICSE 2025 SEIPAt Beko, 73.8% of AI comments were resolved, but average closure time rose from 5h52m to 8h20m[PR] [Grad seminar]
Tantithamthavorn et al., "RovoDev Code Reviewer", ICSE 2026 SEIPAt Atlassian, 38.7% of AI comments led to changes vs 44.45% of human comments; PR cycle time fell 30.8%. Read with Cihan for the opposite result[PR/Ind] [Grad seminar]
Sun et al., "BitsAI-CR" and "Does AI Code Review Lead to Code Changes?"Generate-then-filter reached 75% precision; across 22,000+ comments, concise, snippet-bearing or manually triggered comments led to changes more often[Pre]
GitHub, "60 million Copilot code reviews and counting"29% of reviews deliberately say nothing; a stronger model gave +6% positive feedback for +16% latency[Vendor]
Peralta et al., "Why Are Agentic Pull Requests Merged or Rejected?", MSR 2026Only 35.7% of rejections were clear agent failures; "rejected" is a noisy label for "bad"[PR]
Li, Dutta & Naik, "IRIS"An LLM inferring taint specifications for CodeQL found 55 of 120 CVEs vs 27 for CodeQL alone[Pre]
Oliveira et al., arXiv 2604.23251, ICSE-SEET 2026AI review in a 100+-student capstone: failed AI reviews fell from 227 to 0 after tool and instruction fixes. Pilot your bot[PR]

Week 10 — Supply Chain and Secure Code ​

Core pick: Vero et al., "BaxBench" (2025).

ReadWhat it showsTags
Vero et al. (above)392 backend tasks with functional tests and end-to-end exploits: the best model was 62% correct, and about half of correct programs were exploitable[Pre] [Grad seminar]
CISA, "Widespread Supply Chain Compromise Impacting npm Ecosystem"Shai-Hulud compromised 500+ packages; audit lockfiles, pin, rotate, phishing-resistant MFA[Gov]
Siddiq et al., "Security in the Age of AI Teammates"About 4% of 33,000+ agentic PRs are security-related; they merge less often and wait longer[Pre]
Package hallucination: CSA note on the USENIX Security 2025 study and the 2026 re-test21.7% / 5.2% invented names in 2025; 4.6–6.1% in 2026 frontier models (case B5)[Pre]

ReadWhat it showsTags

OpenAI reports 100% schema adherence[PR] / [Vendor] [Replicable]

Week 12 — Evals and Benchmarks ​

Core pick: Hamel Husain, "Using LLM-as-a-Judge For Evaluation: A Complete Guide" (revised 2026).

ReadWhat it showsTags
Husain (above)Binary pass/fail labels with critiques from one expert; about 100 labels per failure mode; report true-positive and true-negative rates[Pract]
Shankar et al., "Who Validates the Validators?", UIST 2024Criteria drift: grading outputs changes the criteria[PR] [Grad seminar]
Thakur et al., "Judging the Judges", GEM² at ACL 2025Among 13 judges only the largest approached humans; high percent agreement hid large score gaps, so report κ[PR] [Replicable]
Wang, Guo, Gao et al., "Can LLMs Replace Human Evaluators?", ISSTA 2025For code, the best judges correlated with humans at r ≈ 0.81 (translation) and 0.69 (generation)[PR]
Anthropic, "Demystifying evals for AI agents"; Miller, "Adding Error Bars to Evals"Capability vs regression evals; grade outcomes, not step sequences; standard errors, paired comparisons and power[Vendor] / [Pre]
OpenAI, "Why SWE-bench Verified no longer measures frontier coding capabilities"The co-creator stopped reporting it, citing contamination and flawed tests[Ind]
Deng et al., "SWE-Bench Pro"; Gabor, "SWE-Bench Pro is even worse"1,865 contamination-resistant tasks; a hand audit of 100 found lenient and leaking tasks[Pre] / [Pract]
Aleithan et al., "SWE-Bench+"Solution leakage and weak tests cut a resolve rate from 12.47% to 3.97%[Pre]
Wang, Pradel & Liu, "Are 'Solved Issues' in SWE-bench Really Solved Correctly?"29.6% of plausible patches behave differently from the ground truth; 6.2 points of inflation[Pre] [Grad seminar]
Liang et al., "The SWE-Bench Illusion"Models name the buggy file from the issue alone 76% of the time on SWE-bench vs 53% elsewhere[Pre] [Grad seminar] [Replicable]
Zhu et al., "Establishing Best Practices for Building Rigorous Agentic Benchmarks"The Agentic Benchmark Checklist: flaws distort scores by up to 100% relative[Pre] [Grad seminar]
SWE-bench-Live (arXiv 2505.23419), SWE-rebench (2505.20411), LiveCodeBench (2403.07974), SWE-bench Multimodal (2410.03859)Refreshed and date-windowed benchmarks[Pre]

Week 13 — Parallel and Background Agents ​

Core pick: Peralta et al., "Why Are Agentic Pull Requests Merged or Rejected? An Empirical Study" (MSR 2026), read for what a merge rate does and doesn't measure before your team computes its own.

The evidence on parallel agents is thin: courses teach worktrees and orchestration, but none has published outcome data (research notes). That makes week 13's own measurement of review time worth doing carefully.

ReadWhat it showsTags
Peralta et al. (above)In 717 cases inspected by hand (from 9,799 reviewed agent PRs), only 35.7% of rejections were clear agent failures; 31.2% came from workflow constraints, and 33.1% had no visible rationale[PR]
Murphy-Hill, Butler & Savelieva, "Adoption and Impact of Command-Line AI Coding Agents" (on week 2's list)At Microsoft, adopters merged about 24% more PRs than their counterfactual for four months; adoption spread through social networks[Pre/Ind]
Robbes et al., "Agentic Much?"22.2–28.7% of 128,018 GitHub projects had adopted agents[Pre]
UT Austin CS 378, schedule"Parallelize a task once you can name its merge point"[Docs]

Week 14 — Evidence and Responsibility ​

Core pick: one productivity study, its follow-up, and one maintainer policy: Becker et al., the METR randomized trial with experienced open-source developers; METR's "uplift update" (Feb 2026), on why the follow-up's own estimates are unreliable; and LLVM's AI Tool Policy.

ReadWhat it showsTags
Becker et al. (above)The METR randomized trial (collected by the sibling course); an alternate seminar paper[Pre] (alternate)
Cui, Demirer, Jaffe, Musolff, Peng & Salz, "The Effects of Generative AI on High-Skilled Work", Management Science 2026Randomized rollouts for 4,867 developers: +26.08% completed tasks (SE 10.3%); juniors +27–39%, seniors +8–13%; quality not observed[PR] [Grad seminar]
Paradis et al. (Google), "How much does AI impact development speed?"96 engineers; best estimate about 21% less time, wide CI[Pre/Ind]
Lee et al. (Microsoft Research), "The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking", CHI 2025319 knowledge workers: confidence in the AI predicted less critical thinking; self-confidence predicted more[PR] [Grad seminar]
Balepur et al., "(Im)Paired Programming"54 students finished faster with an agent but understood less, and still preferred agents[Pre]
Shen & Tamkin, skill-formation RCTLower quiz scores when learning with AI (collected by the sibling course); an alternate seminar paper[Pre] (alternate)
Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, "Canaries in the Coal Mine?" follow-upEmployment of developers aged 22–25 down nearly 20% from the late-2022 peak; significant only after 2024 under stricter controls[Pre]
Yu & Moon, "Who Will Become the Next Senior?", AIES 2026; Baltes, Cheong & Treude, "An Endless Stream of AI Slop""Absorption" of entry-level work; review friction and collective deskilling[PR] / [Pre]
QEMU, code provenance; Ghostty, AI_POLICY.md; Linux, coding-assistants.rst; Django, AI-assisted contributionsThe range of maintainer policies (section E)[Docs]
Hora & Robbes, 118 contributor AI policies78% allow AI-assisted contributions, 51% require disclosure, 74% a human in the loop[Pre]
Xu, Gao, He & Zhou, "LiCoEval", ICSE 2025Even the best of 14 LLMs produced 0.88–2.01% of outputs "strikingly similar" to existing OSS code, and gave inaccurate licence information, especially for copyleft[PR] [Grad seminar]
Horikawa et al., "Agent or Human?"Detecting agent commits reaches precision 0.332 at realistic prevalence: detection "cannot replace recorded attribution"[Pre]
Thaler v. Perlmutter, cert. denied (SCOTUSblog); Bartz v. Anthropic settlement (JURIST)The human-authorship requirement stands; training on lawfully bought books was fair use, keeping 7M+ pirated ones was not ($1.5B settlement)[Legal]

Replicable studies ​

The five [Replicable] items, with the replication the evidence notes sketch. Each fits about four weeks at $0 using open-weight models on the lab machine and public data, with no human subjects (STUDY_PROTOCOL.md template).

StudyA $0 replication
Liang et al., memorization probeRun the file-path probe with 2–3 open-weight models on 200 SWE-bench Verified issues and 200 post-cutoff SWE-rebench or SWE-bench-Live issues; compare with bootstrap confidence intervals
Zhao, Zhou & Cohen, coverage and mutation vs real bugsGenerate pytest suites with one local model for 10 BugsInPy projects (buggy and fixed versions); measure coverage.py and mutmut scores; correlate both with real-bug detection
Chowdhury et al., code-review agents vs humansRepeat the merge-rate and signal analysis on a newer AIDev or GH Archive slice; hand-code 100 comments with two raters (yourselves) and report κ
Tam et al. vs .txt, structured outputWith two local 7–8B models and grammar-constrained decoding, cross prompt (matched/mismatched) × decoding (free/constrained) × parser (regex/LLM) on GSM8K and Last Letter
Thakur et al., small-judge alignmentRerun the comparison on the paper's released labels with 3–4 small open-weight judges; add one bias test (answer order or verbosity); report κ alongside percent agreement
